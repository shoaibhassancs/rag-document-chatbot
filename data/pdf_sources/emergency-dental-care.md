# Emergency Dental Care

What counts as an emergency and what to do

## Our emergency service

BrightSmile Dental keeps same-day appointment times free every working morning for dental emergencies. If you are an existing patient, you can reach a dentist at any hour of the day or night on our emergency line.

- Emergency line, existing patients (24 hours, every day): (555) 014-2299
- Front desk, during opening hours: (555) 014-2200

If you are not yet a patient, call the front desk. We will do our best to see you the same day, and an emergency visit is a good way to become a patient.

## What counts as a dental emergency

Please call us the same day if you have:

- severe toothache that does not settle with over-the-counter pain relief;
- a knocked-out tooth;
- a broken or cracked tooth with pain or sharp edges;
- swelling of the gums, face or jaw;
- a bleeding mouth that will not stop;
- a lost crown, filling or bridge with pain;
- a dental abscess, which looks like a pimple on the gum and tastes unpleasant;
- an injury to the mouth, lips or tongue.

These can usually wait for a normal appointment: a small chip with no pain, a lost filling with no pain, mild sensitivity, or a dull ache that comes and goes. Call the front desk and we will fit you in as early as we can.

## When to go to the hospital instead

Go to the nearest emergency room or call 911 if you have:

- difficulty breathing or swallowing;
- swelling that is spreading towards your eye or neck;
- a fever with facial swelling;
- heavy bleeding that will not stop after 20 minutes of firm pressure;
- a severe injury to the head or jaw, or you think your jaw is broken.

## First aid for a knocked-out tooth

Time matters. A permanent tooth that is put back within 60 minutes has the best chance of surviving.

- Pick the tooth up by the crown (the white part), never by the root.
- If it is dirty, rinse it very briefly with milk or water. Do not scrub it and do not use soap.
- If you can, gently push it back into the socket and hold it there by biting on a clean cloth.
- If you cannot, keep it in a small container of milk. Do not store it in water.
- Call the emergency line at once.

Baby teeth that are knocked out should not be put back. Call us and we will check the area.

## First aid for other emergencies

- Toothache: rinse with warm water and floss gently to remove trapped food. Do not put aspirin on the gum, as it burns the tissue.
- Broken tooth: rinse your mouth, keep any pieces in milk and use a cold pack on the cheek to reduce swelling.
- Lost crown: keep the crown. You can place it back with dental cement from a pharmacy as a short-term measure. Do not use household glue.
- Swelling: use a cold pack on the outside of the face, 20 minutes on and 20 minutes off.
- Bleeding after an injury: press firmly with clean gauze for 15 minutes.
- Object stuck between teeth: use floss. Do not use a pin or a sharp tool.

## What an emergency visit includes

At an emergency visit, your dentist will:

- ask about your symptoms and your medical history;
- take any X-rays needed;
- treat the problem if it can be fixed safely on the day, for example by smoothing, bonding, cementing a crown or draining an abscess;
- give you written instructions and a plan for any follow-up.

If your problem needs more than one visit, we will relieve your pain first and book the remaining treatment at a convenient time.

## Emergency fees

The emergency exam fee is on our Services and Prices list. Treatment on the day is charged at the usual price. An after-hours emergency visit, outside opening hours, adds a $150 call-out fee. We always tell you the cost before treatment starts, unless you cannot give your consent.

The cancellation fee is waived when you miss a normal appointment because of a dental or medical emergency.

## Staying prepared

- Save (555) 014-2299 in your phone today.
- Keep a small dental emergency kit at home with gauze, a small container with a lid, over-the-counter pain relief and a cold pack.
- Wear a mouthguard for contact sports. We make custom ones.
- Do not chew ice, hard candy or pen caps, and never open packaging with your teeth.
