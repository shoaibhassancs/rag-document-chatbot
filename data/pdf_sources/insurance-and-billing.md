## Plans we do not accept

We are not in-network with the following, and we cannot bill them directly:

- DeltaCare USA and other HMO dental plans, including Humana Dental HMO
- Kaiser dental plans
- Medicaid and other state dental assistance programs

## Out-of-network plans

If your plan is not on the accepted list, you are still welcome as a patient. You pay in full at the visit and we give you an itemized claim form with the codes your insurer needs. Your insurer then reimburses you directly, at whatever out-of-network rate your plan allows. We cannot predict that rate.

## Before your first visit

Please bring your insurance card and a photo ID. If you give us your plan details at least 5 business days before your visit, we will check your benefits first and tell you what to expect.

A benefits check tells us what your plan says it will pay. It is an estimate and not a guarantee. Your insurer makes the final decision when it processes the claim.

## How dental benefits usually work

Plans differ, so always check your own. Many plans follow a pattern like this:

- Preventive care (exams, cleanings, X-rays): often covered at 100 percent.
- Basic care (fillings, simple extractions): often covered at around 80 percent.
- Major care (crowns, root canals, dentures, implants): often covered at around 50 percent.

Your plan may also have:

- an annual maximum, which is the most the insurer will pay in one benefit year;
- a deductible, which you pay first before the insurer pays anything;
- waiting periods for major treatment on new plans;
- frequency limits, such as two cleanings per year;
- a missing tooth clause, which can exclude replacing a tooth lost before you joined the plan.

Orthodontic treatment, cosmetic treatment and whitening are often excluded or paid at a lower rate.

## What you pay at the visit

For in-network plans, you pay your estimated share at the end of each visit. This is your deductible, any co-payment and any amount your plan does not cover. If your insurer later pays less than estimated, we will send you a statement for the difference. If it pays more, we will credit your account.

## How we file claims

- We submit your claim within 3 business days of your visit.
- Insurers typically take 2 to 4 weeks to respond.
- If a claim is denied, we will explain why. On request we will appeal once at no charge.

## Two insurance plans

If you have two plans, tell us at your first visit. We will bill the primary plan first, then send the remaining balance to the secondary plan. The order is set by the plans themselves. For children covered by both parents, the plan of the parent whose birthday falls first in the calendar year is usually the primary plan.

## Statements and due dates

- You receive a statement by email each month if you have a balance.
- Balances are due within 30 days of the statement date.
- A balance that is more than 60 days overdue will have a $15 late fee added.
- After 90 days, we will contact you to arrange payment before any further non-urgent treatment.

If you cannot pay in full, please talk to us early. Our Payment Plans guide explains the options we offer.

## Credit balances and refunds

If you overpay, or your insurer pays more than expected, the credit stays on your account for future visits. If you would rather have a refund, ask the billing team. Refunds are made within 30 days to the original method of payment.

## Itemized receipts, HSA and FSA

We provide an itemized receipt for every visit. It lists each procedure, date and fee, and it works for HSA and FSA reimbursement, for tax records and for out-of-network claims. You can ask for receipts from past years and we will email them to you.

## Estimates for planned treatment

For any treatment plan over $500, we will give you a written estimate before treatment starts. The estimate shows the fee, the expected insurance payment and your expected share. You will not be asked to start treatment on the day without having seen it, except in an emergency.
