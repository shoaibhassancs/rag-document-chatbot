# After Your Tooth Extraction

Instructions for the first week

## The first hour

A blood clot forms in the empty socket. This clot is the natural bandage that protects the bone and starts healing, and the most important thing you can do is protect it.

- Keep the gauze pad we placed over the site and bite down gently for 30 to 45 minutes.
- If the site is still bleeding afterwards, replace it with a fresh damp gauze pad or a moist black tea bag and bite down for another 30 minutes.
- Do not keep changing the gauze every few minutes. Leave it undisturbed.
- Some pink-tinged saliva is normal for the first 24 hours.

## What to eat and drink after a tooth extraction

For the first 24 hours, eat only soft, cool or lukewarm foods. Good choices are:

- yogurt, pudding and applesauce;
- mashed potatoes and soft scrambled eggs;
- lukewarm soup and broth (not hot);
- smoothies and milkshakes, eaten with a spoon;
- ice cream and mashed banana;
- cottage cheese and oatmeal.

Drink plenty of water. Wait until the numbness has worn off before you eat, so you do not bite your cheek or tongue.

Avoid these foods and drinks:

- hot drinks and hot soup for the first 24 hours;
- crunchy, chewy or hard foods such as chips, nuts, popcorn, crusty bread and raw vegetables;
- foods with small seeds or grains, such as sesame, berries with seeds and rice, which can lodge in the socket;
- spicy and acidic foods for the first 3 days;
- alcohol for at least 48 hours, and for as long as you take prescription pain medication;
- carbonated drinks for the first 24 hours.

From day 2 you can add softer solid foods such as pasta, fish and well-cooked vegetables. Chew on the opposite side of your mouth. Most people return to a normal diet within 7 to 10 days, when the site feels comfortable.

## What not to do for the first 72 hours

- Do not use a straw. Suction can pull the blood clot out.
- Do not spit or rinse forcefully.
- Do not smoke or vape. If you can, stay smoke-free for at least 5 days. Smoking is the biggest risk factor for a painful dry socket.
- Do not touch the site with your tongue or fingers.
- Do not do hard exercise. Raised blood pressure can restart bleeding.

## Pain and swelling

Some discomfort for 2 to 3 days is normal. Swelling usually peaks about 48 hours after surgery and then goes down.

- Take your first pain tablet before the numbness wears off, with food.
- Ibuprofen is usually the first choice unless you cannot take it. Take it as directed on the box and by Dr. Patel or Dr. Okafor.
- If we prescribed pain medication, take it exactly as instructed. Do not drive after taking it.
- Use an ice pack on the outside of your cheek, 20 minutes on and 20 minutes off, for the first 24 hours.
- After 48 hours, switch to a warm compress to ease stiffness in the jaw.
- Keep your head raised on an extra pillow for the first two nights.

## Keeping your mouth clean

- For the first 24 hours, do not rinse. Brush your other teeth gently and avoid the extraction site.
- After 24 hours, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals: half a teaspoon of salt in a glass of warm water. Let the water fall out of your mouth. Do not spit.
- From day 3, brush normally, but go slowly around the site.
- We may give you a syringe for gently cleaning the socket after about 5 to 7 days. Use it only if we tell you to.

## Stitches

If we placed stitches, they dissolve by themselves in 7 to 10 days. Do not pull on them. If we used stitches that do not dissolve, we will remove them at a short follow-up visit.

## Dry socket

Dry socket happens when the blood clot is lost too early and the bone is exposed. It most often appears 3 to 5 days after the extraction. Signs are:

- severe pain that gets worse instead of better;
- pain spreading to your ear, eye or neck;
- a bad taste or smell;
- a visibly empty-looking socket.

Dry socket is uncomfortable but treatable. Call us and we will see you quickly and place a medicated dressing.

## When to call us

Call the clinic on (555) 014-2200, or the emergency line (555) 014-2299 outside opening hours, if you have:

- bleeding that does not stop after 60 minutes of firm pressure with fresh gauze;
- a temperature above 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit (38 degrees Celsius);
- swelling that grows after the third day;
- pus or a bad taste that does not go away;
- an allergic reaction, such as a rash or itching, after taking medication;
- numbness that has not worn off after 12 hours.

If you have difficulty breathing or swallowing, or swelling spreading to your eye or neck, call 911 immediately.

## Your follow-up

We will usually see you again about 7 to 10 days after your extraction to check healing. If you are planning an implant or a bridge, we will discuss it then.
