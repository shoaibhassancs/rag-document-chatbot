# Children's Dentistry

## When should my child first see a dentist?

We recommend a first visit when the first tooth appears, and no later than your child's first birthday. An early visit is short and gentle. It helps your child get used to the clinic and lets us teach you how to look after those first teeth.

## Who sees children?

Dr. Lena Brandt, who is trained in pediatric dentistry, sees children on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays. Our other dentists also see older children and teenagers. Children's appointments are made in our brighter treatment room, which has a cartoon ceiling and a toy box.

## How we work with young patients

- We use simple, friendly words: the suction is "Mr. Thirsty" and the polish is "tickle toothpaste".
- We show your child each tool before we use it.
- For babies and toddlers we use a "knee-to-knee" exam: you sit facing the dentist with your child lying with their head in the dentist's lap and their legs around you.
- We praise good behavior with stickers and a small prize from the treasure chest.
- We never force a child to open their mouth. If they are too upset, we stop and try again another day.

## Staying with your child

For children under 13, a parent or guardian must stay at the clinic for the whole appointment. You are welcome in the treatment room. Older children can choose whether they would like you with them. A guardian must give written consent for any treatment on a child under 18.

## Routine care for children

- Exams and cleanings every 6 months.
- Fluoride varnish to strengthen the enamel.
- Dental sealants on the biting surfaces of the back teeth, usually when the first permanent molars appear at about age 6 and the second set at about age 12. Sealants protect against decay.
- X-rays only when needed. Digital X-rays use low doses of radiation.
- A custom sports mouthguard for any child who plays contact sports.

## Common questions about baby teeth

- Baby teeth matter. They save space for the adult teeth and help with speaking and eating. Decay in baby teeth can spread and cause pain and infection.
- Baby teeth start to fall out at about age 6 and the last ones go at about 12.
- A wobbly tooth can be left to come out on its own. Let your child wiggle it gently.
- If a baby tooth is knocked out, do not put it back. Call us.

## Thumb sucking and pacifiers

Most children stop by themselves between ages 2 and 4. If the habit continues after the adult teeth start to come in, it can change the shape of the mouth. Praise works better than scolding. Ask us for help if you are worried.

## Brushing for children

- Under 3: brush twice a day with a smear of fluoride toothpaste, about the size of a grain of rice.
- Ages 3 to 6: a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste.
- Help children brush until they can tie their own shoelaces, at about age 7 or 8.
- Start flossing as soon as two teeth touch.

## Diet

Limit sugary drinks, including juice and sports drinks, and offer them with meals only. Water is the best drink between meals. Never put a baby to bed with a bottle of milk or juice.

## Teenagers

Teenagers have their own needs: wisdom teeth, braces, whitening, sports injuries and the temptation of energy drinks. Our dentists speak to teenagers directly and with respect, and we involve parents as the young person prefers.

## Fear of the dentist

If your child is anxious, tell us when you book. We can plan a short "happy visit" with no treatment. Nitrous oxide (laughing gas) is available for children who need help to relax. See the Sedation Options guide.
