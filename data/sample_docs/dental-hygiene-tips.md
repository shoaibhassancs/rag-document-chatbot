# Dental Hygiene Tips

## Brushing

- Brush twice a day, for 2 minutes each time: after breakfast and before bed.
- Use a soft-bristled brush and fluoride toothpaste. A small, pea-sized amount is enough.
- Hold the brush at a 45 degree angle to the gumline and use short, gentle circles. Scrubbing hard damages the gums and wears away the enamel.
- Brush every surface: outside, inside and the chewing surfaces.
- Brush your tongue lightly to reduce bacteria and bad breath.
- Spit out the toothpaste, but do not rinse with water. The fluoride works longer if you leave a thin film on your teeth.
- Replace your toothbrush, or brush head, every 3 months or sooner if the bristles are bent.
- Electric toothbrushes with a timer can help you brush for the full 2 minutes and with the right pressure.

## Flossing

- Clean between your teeth once a day, because a toothbrush cannot reach these places.
- Wrap about 45 centimeters (18 inches) of floss around your fingers and slide it gently between the teeth, curving it into a C-shape against each tooth.
- If floss is hard to use, try floss picks, interdental brushes or a water flosser.
- A little bleeding in the first week or two is common and should stop as your gums become healthier.

## Mouthwash

Mouthwash is an extra, not a replacement for brushing and flossing. A fluoride mouthwash can help if you are prone to cavities. Choose an alcohol-free one if you have a dry mouth.

## Food and drink

- Sugar feeds the bacteria that cause decay. The number of times you have sugar matters more than the amount, so limit snacking and sipping sugary drinks.
- Water is the best drink. Drinking water after meals helps to rinse your mouth.
- Fizzy drinks, including sugar-free ones, and sports drinks are acidic and can wear away enamel. Drink them with meals and do not sip them for hours.
- Wait 30 minutes after eating or drinking something acidic before you brush.
- Crunchy fruit and vegetables, cheese and plain yogurt are good for your teeth.
- Chewing sugar-free gum after meals increases saliva, which helps to protect teeth.

## Tobacco and vaping

Smoking and vaping raise your risk of gum disease, tooth loss, stained teeth and oral cancer, and they slow healing after treatment. We will gladly help you quit.

## Protecting your teeth

- Wear a mouthguard for contact sports.
- Do not chew ice, pens or hard candy, or open bottles with your teeth.
- If you grind your teeth at night, ask us about a night guard. Signs include sore jaw muscles in the morning and flat, worn teeth.

## Dry mouth

A dry mouth raises the risk of decay. It is a side effect of many medicines. Sip water often, use sugar-free lozenges and tell us about it.

## Regular check-ups

Even if your teeth feel fine, a check-up every 6 months lets us find problems early, when they are small, simple and cheaper to treat. Cavities and gum disease often cause no pain at the beginning.

## Warning signs

Contact us if you notice:

- gums that bleed easily, are swollen or are receding;
- persistent bad breath;
- sensitivity to hot and cold;
- a loose tooth;
- a sore, patch or lump in your mouth that does not heal in two weeks.
