# After Your Filling

## Numbness

The local anesthetic usually wears off in 2 to 4 hours. Until then, your lip, cheek and tongue may feel numb.

- Do not eat or chew until the numbness has gone. It is easy to bite your cheek or tongue without noticing.
- Avoid very hot drinks while you are still numb, as you cannot judge the temperature.
- If you have a child with a filling, watch them to make sure they do not chew on their lip.

## Eating

Our tooth-colored composite fillings are hardened with a blue light, so they are fully set when you leave the clinic. You can chew on the new filling as soon as the numbness has gone, although we suggest you avoid very hard or sticky foods such as hard candy, caramel and ice for the first day, to give your tooth time to settle.

## Sensitivity

It is normal for the tooth to feel sensitive to hot, cold, sweet foods and biting pressure for a week or two. This is more common if the cavity was deep.

- Use toothpaste for sensitive teeth and brush gently.
- Avoid very cold or very hot drinks for a few days.
- Over-the-counter pain relief can help with any soreness.
- Sensitivity should get better each week.

## Your gums

Your gums around the filling may be sore for a few days because of the dental dam or the clamp. Rinse with warm salt water, half a teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water, 2 or 3 times a day.

## Your bite

Gently bite your teeth together once the numbness has worn off. If your bite feels high, or the filling feels in the way, call us. A quick adjustment of a few minutes fixes this and prevents a sore tooth later.

## Looking after your filling

- Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste and floss every day, including around the filled tooth.
- Do not use your teeth to open packets or bite your nails.
- If you clench or grind your teeth at night, ask us about a night guard.
- Keep your regular check-ups. We check the edges of fillings at every exam.

## When to call us

Call the clinic on (555) 014-2200 if you have:

- pain that gets worse after the first week, or pain that keeps you awake;
- a bite that still feels uneven after a day or two;
- a filling that feels rough, sharp or loose, or that has a piece missing;
- swelling near the tooth.

## How long do fillings last?

A well-kept composite filling usually lasts 7 to 10 years, sometimes longer. A larger filling may wear sooner. If a large part of the tooth is missing, we may recommend a crown, which gives the tooth more protection.
