# New Patient Checklist

Welcome. This checklist helps you get ready for your first visit.

## Before your visit

- Complete your new patient forms online. The link is in your confirmation email. This saves about 15 minutes.
- Check that you have your insurance details ready. If you have insurance, send us the plan name, the member ID and the group number at least 5 business days before your visit, so that we can check your benefits.
- Write down any questions or concerns, and anything that has been troubling you, such as pain, sensitivity or a chipped tooth.
- If you have recent dental X-rays from another dentist, ask them to send them to us, or bring a copy. This could save you from repeat X-rays.

## What to bring

- Photo identification, such as a driver's license.
- Your insurance card, if you have one.
- A list of your medicines, including doses, vitamins and supplements.
- The name and phone number of your doctor, in case we need to speak to them.
- Your payment method.
- Any dental appliances you wear, such as a night guard or a retainer.
- For a child: the child's insurance card, and details of any health conditions, and a parent or guardian.

## On the day

- Please arrive 10 minutes before your appointment. If you could not complete the forms online, arrive 25 minutes early.
- Brush and floss your teeth beforehand if you can.
- Eat normally. There is no need to fast for a routine first visit.
- Wear comfortable clothes. We have blankets if you feel cold.
- Turn off your phone, or put it on silent.

## What happens during the visit

Your first visit includes a full examination, digital X-rays, an oral cancer screening and, when your gums are healthy enough, a professional cleaning. After that, we talk through what we found and give you a written treatment plan. The Patient Welcome Guide describes every step.

## Health conditions to tell us about

Please tell us if you have, or have had:

- heart conditions, including a heart murmur or joint replacements;
- diabetes, high blood pressure or bleeding disorders;
- allergies, especially to latex, penicillin or local anesthetics;
- a pacemaker or other implanted device;
- pregnancy, or if you think you could be pregnant;
- cancer treatment, including radiation to the head and neck;
- any medicine that thins the blood, or bisphosphonates for bone health.

This helps us look after you safely.

## After your visit

You will receive a written summary of your visit and your treatment plan by email. If you have any questions after you get home, call us or reply to the email.
