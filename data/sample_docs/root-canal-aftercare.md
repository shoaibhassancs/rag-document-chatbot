# After Your Root Canal

## What was done

A root canal removes the infected or inflamed nerve tissue from inside your tooth, cleans the space and seals it. This stops the pain and saves the tooth. Dr. Okafor performs most of our root canals.

## Numbness

Your mouth will be numb for 2 to 4 hours. Do not eat or chew until the numbness is gone. Be careful not to bite your lip, cheek or tongue.

## Soreness

Mild to moderate soreness for 3 to 5 days is normal. The tissue around the tip of the root has been irritated, and the tooth may feel tender when you bite.

- Take over-the-counter anti-inflammatory medicine, such as ibuprofen, as directed on the pack, with food.
- If we prescribed medicine, take it as instructed.
- A cold pack on the cheek can help for the first day.
- Rinse with warm salt water, half a teaspoon in a cup of warm water, 2 or 3 times a day.

## Protecting the tooth until the crown

Most root canal teeth need a crown. Without a crown the tooth is more likely to crack. Until your permanent crown or final filling is placed, usually 2 to 3 weeks later:

- Do not bite on the treated tooth. Chew on the other side.
- Avoid hard, crunchy and sticky foods, such as nuts, chewy candy and ice.
- The temporary filling is soft. If it comes out, call us. It does not mean the treatment has failed, but the tooth needs protection.
- Keep brushing and flossing around the tooth. Floss gently, and pull the floss out sideways to avoid dislodging the temporary filling.

## Antibiotics

Antibiotics are not routinely needed after a root canal. We prescribe them only if there is spreading infection, swelling or a fever. If we do, finish the whole course.

## What is normal and what is not

Normal:

- tenderness on biting for a few days;
- a feeling that the tooth is a little "different";
- a slightly sore jaw from keeping your mouth open.

Call us promptly if you have:

- severe pain that is not relieved by pain medicine;
- swelling of your face, gum or neck;
- a fever;
- an allergic reaction, such as a rash or itching;
- a bite that feels high, even after the numbness has gone;
- the temporary filling falling out.

If you have trouble breathing or swallowing, call 911.

## Your follow-up

You will come back for your crown or final restoration about 2 to 3 weeks after treatment. We may also take an X-ray at 6 to 12 months to check healing.

## How long will it last?

A root canal tooth with a good crown can last a lifetime with proper care. Brush, floss and have regular check-ups, just like your other teeth.
