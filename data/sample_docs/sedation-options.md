# Sedation Options

## You do not have to be afraid

If thoughts of the dentist make you tense, you are far from alone. We offer two kinds of sedation, and a friendly, unhurried approach that many anxious patients find is all they need. Prices are on the Services and Prices list.

## Option 1: nitrous oxide (laughing gas)

Nitrous oxide is a mix of oxygen and a gas that helps you feel calm and a little detached. You breathe it through a small mask over your nose.

- It starts to work within a few minutes.
- You stay awake and can talk to the dentist.
- It wears off within 5 to 10 minutes after the mask is removed, and you breathe pure oxygen at the end.
- Most patients can drive themselves home afterwards.
- It is suitable for children and adults. Dr. Brandt uses it often with young patients.
- Please have only a light meal beforehand. A very full stomach can cause nausea.
- It is not recommended if you have a severe cold or a blocked nose, some lung conditions or if you are in the first trimester of pregnancy. Tell us about your health first.

## Option 2: oral sedation

Oral sedation uses a prescribed tablet that you take before your appointment. It makes you deeply relaxed and drowsy. Many patients do not remember much of the visit.

- You take the tablet at the clinic, about an hour before treatment begins, after we check your health.
- You will be awake but very relaxed, and you can respond to the dentist.
- You must have an adult to drive you to the clinic and home, and to stay with you for the rest of the day. We will not start if you arrive alone.
- Do not eat solid food for 6 hours before your appointment. You may drink clear fluids up to 2 hours before.
- Do not drink alcohol for 24 hours before and after.
- You must not drive, work, sign legal papers or look after children for 24 hours.
- Oral sedation is available for patients aged 12 and over.
- Appointments with oral sedation need 48 hours' notice if you need to cancel or move them. See the Cancellation Policy.

## What about IV sedation and general anesthesia?

We do not offer intravenous (IV) sedation or general anesthesia at BrightSmile. If you need them, for example for complex wisdom tooth surgery or because of severe dental phobia, we will refer you to an oral surgeon we trust. See our Referral Specialists note.

## Local anesthetic

Sedation does not replace local anesthetic. Your dentist will still numb the area being treated, so you do not feel pain. We apply numbing gel first, and we inject slowly.

## Which option is right for me?

- A light fear, or a short procedure: nitrous oxide is a good first choice.
- Strong anxiety, a long procedure or several treatments in one visit: oral sedation may be better.
- A needle phobia: tell us. We can use nitrous oxide first, before the injection.

## Safety

Before any sedation we review your health, medicines and allergies. We monitor your oxygen levels and pulse throughout. Our team is trained in emergency response, and emergency equipment, including oxygen and a defibrillator, is on site.

## Before you book

Call us for a consultation. We will talk through your worries, your health and your options. There is no pressure, and you can change your mind at any time.
